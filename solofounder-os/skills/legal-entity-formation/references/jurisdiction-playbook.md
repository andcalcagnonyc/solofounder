# Jurisdiction Playbook (starting map)

This file is an orientation map, not a source of law. Rules, fees, thresholds, and timelines change. For the founder's actual jurisdictions, research current rules from the company registry and tax authority, cite them with dates, and have a locally licensed lawyer confirm.

## Questions that decide the structure

1. Where does the founder live and pay tax? Management and control from that country can create tax residence or a taxable presence for a foreign company.
2. Who will invest, and what structure do those investors expect?
3. Where are the first customers, and do they require a local contracting entity?
4. Where will people be hired?
5. How much administrative load can one person carry? Two entities double it.

## Common startup vehicles by country

| Country | Typical vehicle | Things to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | Delaware C-Corporation (venture track); LLC (bootstrapped) | Registered agent, foreign qualification in the operating state, franchise tax, founder stock election deadline, federal tax ID, beneficial-ownership reporting |
| United Kingdom | Private limited company (Ltd) | Registered office, people with significant control register, confirmation statement, investor relief schemes |
| Italy | S.r.l. / S.r.l. semplificata; innovative startup status | Notary or online route, certified email and digital signature, business register, VAT number, innovative-startup requirements |
| Germany | GmbH; UG (haftungsbeschränkt) | Notarisation, share capital, commercial register, trade registration, transparency register |
| France | SAS / SASU | Share-capital deposit, registration through the single portal, young-innovative-company status |
| Spain | S.L. | Notary, tax ID, commercial registry, startup-law certification |
| Netherlands | B.V. | Notarial deed, chamber of commerce, beneficial-owner register |
| Ireland | Private company limited by shares (LTD) | Resident-director or bond requirement, registry filings |
| Estonia | OÜ (often via e-Residency) | Contact person and legal address, where management actually takes place |
| Switzerland | GmbH / AG | Share capital, resident signatory, notary |
| Canada | Federal or provincial corporation | Director residency rules by jurisdiction, extra-provincial registration, small-business tax status |
| Singapore | Private limited company (Pte. Ltd.) | Locally resident director, company secretary, registered address |
| India | Private limited company; LLP | Director identification, resident director, foreign-investment rules, startup recognition |
| United Arab Emirates | Free-zone company; mainland LLC | Free zone choice, activity licence, visa and substance requirements, corporate tax registration |
| Australia | Proprietary limited company (Pty Ltd) | Resident director, business number, director identification |
| Israel | Private company (Ltd) | Registrar filing, tax file opening, innovation-authority programs |
| Brazil | Sociedade Limitada (Ltda.) | Resident administrator, tax registration, foreign-capital registration |
| Japan | Kabushiki Kaisha (KK); Godo Kaisha (GK) | Articles notarisation, capital deposit, resident representative practice |

For any country not listed, apply the same method: identify the standard limited-liability vehicle founders and local investors use, then research formation steps and ongoing obligations.

## Holding-company patterns

- **Local entity only**: simplest; preferred unless investors require otherwise.
- **Foreign parent with local subsidiary** (for example a parent in a venture-standard jurisdiction owning the operating company where the founder lives): familiar to international investors; adds intercompany agreements, transfer pricing, and two sets of filings.
- **Flip later**: start local, restructure when an investor requires it; check the tax cost of the restructuring before relying on it.

## Formation checklist (generic)

1. Name check and reservation
2. Constitutional documents
3. Directors, officers, registered office, company secretary or agent where required
4. Share capital and founder subscription
5. Registry filing and, where required, notarisation
6. Beneficial-ownership filing
7. Tax identification and registrations
8. Bank account and capital deposit
9. Statutory registers and minute book
10. First board resolutions: officers, bank mandate, share issuance, IP assignment, financial year
