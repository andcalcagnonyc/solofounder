# Run C2: ops-incident-response (Relay Desk, Inc.)

**Founder request:** "Since this morning's prompt change the agent has been issuing 50 USD refunds on tickets that never asked for one. 61 refunds across 9 customers so far. Help."

## Response to the founder

Marcus, **switch the refund action off for all 38 customers now, then revert the prompt change.** Diagnosis comes after the refunds stop. I am treating this as a SEV1 incident, INC-2026-11-20-01, and have opened the timeline.

None of your tools are connected to me, so I cannot act in GitHub, Datadog, Stripe, or Intercom. You run the steps; I keep the log and draft everything.

### Do these now, in order

1. **Disable refunds everywhere.** Use the kill switch, or set the no-approval refund limit from 50 USD to 0 so every refund waits for a person. If neither exists, revoke the agent's refund credential or pause the agent. Apply it to all customers, not only the 9: "so far" means the count is still rising.
2. **Revert the prompt change** in GitHub (`git revert <commit>` on this morning's prompt commit, or redeploy the previous prompt version) and deploy.
3. **Confirm the stop:** no new agent-issued refunds for 30 minutes in Datadog or your logs. Tell me the time.
4. **Leave refunds off after the revert.** The revert removes the likely cause; it does not prove it was the only one.

Do not, for now: reverse or re-charge any refund, contact any shopper, or delete logs. Those steps are external or cannot be undone, and each affected customer should decide what happens to their shoppers.

### Once it has stopped

**Identify every affected action.** Export all agent-issued refunds from the deploy time to the stop time, for all customers: refund ID, customer, ticket, shopper, amount, time, ticket text, and the agent's reasoning trace. Mark each as requested, not requested, or unclear. Some of today's refunds may be legitimate.

**Investigate with evidence.** Three hypotheses, each with the fact that would support it:
- **The prompt change caused it:** the first bad refund follows the deploy, the diff touches refund guidance, and replaying the 61 tickets refunds on the new prompt but not the old.
- **Something else changed this morning** (model version, tool configuration, the limit setting): the deploy and config history shows a second change.
- **It was already happening at a lower rate:** unrequested refunds appear in the previous 7 days.

Every refund landing at exactly 50 USD suggests the limit became a target. That is worth checking in the diff, and it is a guess until you do.

**Communicate.** Drafts are ready and none is sent without your approval:
- First notice to each of the 9 customers, within an hour of the confirmed stop
- An optional status notice for everyone else
- A follow-up carrying each customer's refund list
- A final message with the review

**Notification duties.** This is a checklist for your lawyer, not legal advice:
- **Customer contracts:** incident-notice, service-level, and liability clauses. Hand-off: `legal-commercial-contracts`.
- **Insurer:** if you hold a technology errors-and-omissions or cyber policy, check its notice deadline before you promise customers money.
- **Payment provider:** terms for whichever account issued the refunds.
- **Regulators:** I see no trigger on the facts given, since no data exposure is reported. If the export shows the agent disclosed or mishandled shopper data, `legal-privacy-and-ai-compliance` assesses it.

### Decisions I need from you

1. **Severity SEV1:** confirm.
2. **Remediation:** credit the 9 customers the full erroneous amount (my recommendation, at most about 3,050 USD on current numbers), or discuss case by case. This is a financial action, so it waits for your yes.
3. **Customer notices:** approve, edit, or hold each draft. Decide whether to publish a status notice to unaffected customers.
4. **Refunds stay on "approval required"** until the evaluation cases, the code-level guard, and the alert are in place.
5. **Outbound campaign:** hold the first send until this incident is closed. The sequence makes claims about control over the agent's actions.

### Questions I need answered

1. Has the refund action been disabled, and at what time? Is there a kill switch?
2. What time was the prompt change deployed, and which commit? Did anything else ship this morning?
3. Through which account does the agent issue refunds: each customer's own store or payment account, or Relay Desk's Stripe? This decides who is out of pocket.
4. How and when did you notice? Is 61 from logs or from customer reports?
5. Were all 61 exactly 50 USD? Any duplicates on one order?
6. Is the rest of the agent's ticket handling behaving normally?
7. Do you have a status page, and what do your contracts say about incident notice?
8. Do you hold insurance that might cover this, and who is your lawyer?

### After recovery

I close the incident only when four checks pass: zero unrequested refunds since the revert; a replay of the 61 tickets on the reverted prompt issues no refund; a refund-rate monitor is live in Datadog; and all 9 customers have their list and a remediation decision.

The blameless review is drafted with the unknowns marked. It proposes eight corrective actions with owners and dates for you to confirm. The main ones:
- Add the 61 tickets to the evaluation set as "must not refund" (by 24 November)
- Add a code-level guard so the refund tool refuses a ticket with no refund request (by 27 November)
- Cap refunds per customer per hour and per day (by 27 November)
- Alert on refund volume in Datadog (by 24 November)
- Gate prompt changes behind evaluations and a staged rollout (by 4 December)

Hand-offs: `support-ticket-triage-and-response` for affected customers' replies, `marketing-communications` if you make a public statement, `legal-privacy-and-ai-compliance` if data turns out to be involved.
