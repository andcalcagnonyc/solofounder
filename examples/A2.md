### What I checked today (20 November 2026)

- **Italian tax residence of companies.** A company is Italian tax resident if any one of three tests is met in Italy for most of the tax period: registered office, place of effective management, or main place of ordinary management. In force from 1 January 2024. Source: [Agenzia delle Entrate press release on Circolare 20/E, 4 November 2024](https://agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/6521409/045_Com.+st.+Circolare+residenza+fiscale+04.11.24/3a0b0a1e-f55f-a11d-eae5-682b9c8bb92c). **Consequence:** a Delaware company run by you alone from Milan would very likely be Italian tax resident too. Incorporating abroad does not move your tax position abroad.
- **Innovative startup status.** After Law 193/2024 the company must be at most five years old, a micro, small, or medium enterprise, without prevalent consulting or agency activity, and meet at least one innovation requirement (for example R&D of at least 15%, or registered software). Italian-taxpayer investors get a 30% deduction, or 65% under de minimis in the first three years. Source: [Camera dei deputati, page dated 10 June 2025](https://temi.camera.it/temi/start-up-e-pmi-innovative.html). The [ministry page](https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/impresa/competitivita-e-nuove-imprese/start-up-innovative) could not be reached today; confirm there. These deductions are against Italian tax, so I assume they do little for US angels.
- **Delaware running cost.** Annual report 50 USD plus franchise tax, due 1 March. Minimum tax is 175 USD (authorized-shares method) or 400 USD (assumed-par-value method); late report penalty 200 USD plus 1.5% interest per month. Source: [Delaware Division of Corporations](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtax/). The two methods can differ widely for a startup, so the lawyer should compute both.
- **US beneficial-ownership reporting.** A FinCEN final rule effective 14 August 2026 permanently exempts US-formed companies. Source: [Morrison Foerster note, 20 August 2026](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260820-fincen-ends-beneficial-ownership-reporting), a secondary source; confirm at fincen.gov.

**Not researched; I state no figures for these.** Consult: Registro Imprese and the Consiglio Nazionale del Notariato (S.r.l. capital, formation route, fees, timeline, beneficial-owner register); the Delaware fee schedule (filing fee, registered agent); irs.gov (tax ID for a non-US founder, annual filings, founder stock election); sec.gov (exemption for angel sales); Agenzia delle Entrate (tax on a later share-for-share restructuring); isap.sejm.gov.pl (Polish contractor and copyright assignment rules).

### Recommendation and what would change it

**Option B, parent-first**, because your plan is a round mostly from US angels in four months. If they need a US vehicle, forming the parent first avoids a later flip, and a restructuring costs least when the company has little value. The S.r.l. gives customers an EU counterparty and holds the operations where they actually are.

Two checks before anything is formed:
1. **Ask at least three likely angels** what vehicle and instrument they need.
2. **Get a written view from an Italian cross-border tax adviser** on the parent's Italian tax residence, your personal position, and which entity should own the IP.

The recommendation changes if:
- the angels will invest in an S.r.l., or the round shifts to Italian or EU investors: choose **A**;
- quotes for B exceed what you can spend before the raise: choose **C**, with the flip costed in advance;
- you relocate or add a US-based director: management may leave Italy, and I would redo the analysis;
- the raise slips well beyond four months: choose **A or C** and revisit at term-sheet stage.

One caution on budget: two entities plus cross-border advice may not fit within 5,000 EUR. I have no quotes yet and will not guess the fees.
